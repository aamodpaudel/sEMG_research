Calibrate and test

  1. Sit about an arm's length from the screen, face lit from the front.
  2. Calibrate: look at each orange dot until it disappears. 9 dots in a 3 × 3 grid, about 15 seconds.
  3. Accuracy test: 5 new dots measure the error in pixels and degrees, and set how far snapping reaches.
  4. Target test: look at the orange square and press Space when it turns teal. 12 trials.
  5. Live snap: the button you look at on this page gets a teal outline.

What gets measured

Accuracy is the distance between where you looked and the Track Model's median estimate. Precision is the jitter between consecutive estimates. Both are reported in pixels and degrees of visual angle, the standard eye-tracking units.

Snapping. Webcam gaze is off by a few degrees, about the size of a button, so Nullpoint never steers a cursor pixel by pixel. It highlights the page element that most likely has your attention, given your own measured error, and only switches when another element clearly wins.

Target test measures what matters for Nullpoint: whether the snapped highlight picks the right one of 12 buttons, compared on the same trials with a plain gaze cursor, and how long it takes.

Gaze narrows, voice picks. The top 3 candidates and their probabilities go to the voice decoder, which chooses among them by the word you say.

Results go to the run log with your screen size and distance.
